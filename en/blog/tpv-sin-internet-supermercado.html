POS without internet: what has to keep working when the connection drops

If the fibre goes down at noon on a Saturday, can your till still take money? What must work offline, how it affects receipt numbering and what VeriFactu says.

posverifactuoperations

Saturday, twelve noon, the fibre goes down. If your POS needs the cloud for every sale, a queue builds and someone starts writing sales on a notepad. If the till keeps sales on the computer itself, the cashier doesn't even notice.

In a demo with good Wi-Fi the two look the same. The difference shows up the day the internet fails.

Two ways to build a POS

Cloud-only. The till is a web page. Every product lookup, every sale and every receipt number comes from the server. It works as long as there is a connection.

Local-first. The till keeps the catalogue, the prices and a sales database on the machine itself. It takes payment without asking anyone and sends sales to the server when it can. The cloud is still there for reports, stock and backups, but it doesn't sit in the middle of every sale.

For a supermarket only the second one makes sense. A till that can't take money is a closed shop.

What has to keep working offline

  • Scanning products and finding their price.
  • Reading scale labels and weighing at the counter.
  • Taking cash and printing the receipt.
  • Numbering receipts with no gaps or duplicates.
  • Saving every sale so it survives someone switching the computer off.

Card payments are a separate matter, because they depend on the card terminal and its own connection. Many terminals have a SIM card and keep working when the shop's fibre is down. Ask your bank which one you have.

Numbering, the question almost nobody asks

In Spain a till receipt is a simplified invoice, and the invoicing regulation requires its numbers to be sequential within each series (article 7.1 of Royal Decree 1619/2012). With two tills and no internet, who decides the next number?

If the server hands out numbers, there are no numbers offline. If each till numbers on its own without coordinating, you get duplicates. Solid systems give each till its own series or reserve blocks of numbers for each terminal in advance.

VeriFactu and outages

Under VeriFactu, the invoicing software sends every record to the Spanish Tax Agency. Many people think that means you must be online at all times. It doesn't.

Order HAC/1177/2024 expects that a technical incident can block submission. When that happens, records are sent "as soon as possible, respecting the time order in which the records were generated" (article 16.4), and they carry an incident flag. What you can't do is skip generating the record. The receipt is issued as normal and the record waits to be sent.

A VeriFactu-ready POS has to keep pending records locally, in order, and send them when the connection is back. If your provider says their till can't sell offline "because of VeriFactu", the regulation doesn't require that.

Five questions for your POS provider

  1. If I unplug the router right now, can I take a sale?
  2. Where is an offline sale stored, and what happens if the power goes right after?
  3. How are receipts numbered when two tills are offline?
  4. When the connection is back, how do I know every sale arrived?
  5. How long can the till stay offline?

You can check the first one in ten seconds during the demo. Ask for it.

How Salut handles it

Salut's desktop till keeps the catalogue and sales in a database on the computer itself. Each sale is saved locally before the receipt prints, so a power cut right after doesn't lose it.

For numbering, each till gets a block of receipt numbers reserved for it in advance. While online, it asks for a new block before it runs out. Offline, it keeps using the block it has, without clashing with the till next to it.

When the internet comes back, the till sends its pending sales to the server and stock and reports catch up.
